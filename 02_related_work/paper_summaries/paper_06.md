# Paper 06 Summary

## Citation

- **Title:** Using Focus to Personalise Learning and Feedback in Software Engineering Education
- **Authors:** Bansri Amish Modi, Andrew Cain, Guy Wood-Bradley, Jake Renzella
- **Year:** 2023
- **Source:** 2023 IEEE/ACM 45th International Conference on Software Engineering: Software Engineering Education and Training (ICSE-SEET)
- **DOI/Link:** [https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSE-SEET58685.2023.00033](https://doi.org/10.1109/ICSE-SEET58685.2023.00033)

## Problem

Software engineering (SE) education involves a wide variety of tasks and topics. Providing timely and personalized feedback is challenging due to large class sizes and the diversity of student backgrounds. Current systems lack a unified mechanism to relate feedback from different tasks, which limits students' ability to see their overall learning trajectory and areas needing focus.

## Method

The authors propose a "Focus" framework that models learning objectives and relates feedback across different software engineering tasks. It helps personalize learning by tracking student performance relative to course learning outcomes (CLOs) and highlighting specific topics (focus areas) that require student attention.

## Context

Higher education software engineering courses, aiming to improve pedagogical feedback loops and student engagement in computing education.

## Key Findings

1. Implementing focus-based personalization helps students better connect feedback from multiple assignments to their overall learning goals.
2. It improves the efficiency of student reflection and self-study, making learning outcomes more transparent.

## Limitations

The framework's success depends on the initial mapping of CLOs to specific course tasks, which requires significant effort from instructors. It was evaluated in a limited number of class cohorts.

## Relevance to our topic

This paper establishes the context of software engineering education where personalized feedback and CLO alignment are highly demanded. It justifies why a system automating this process (our RAG-based LMS) is valuable for addressing instructor workload and student personalization.
