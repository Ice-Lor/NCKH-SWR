# Paper 02 Summary

## Citation

- **Title:** Leveraging In-Context Learning and Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Automatic Question Generation in Educational Domains
- **Authors:** Subhankar Maity, Aniket Deroy, Sudeshna Sarkar
- **Year:** 2025
- **Source:** arXiv
- **Link / DOI:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17397

## Problem

The study addresses the problem of automated educational question generation (AQG) that maintains pedagogical quality and contextual alignment. Traditional question generation methods often produce questions that are detached from the lesson context or lack pedagogical structure. Meanwhile, deep learning fine-tuning approaches (such as T5 or BART) require a massive amount of specialized labeled data, which is extremely scarce in the educational domain. Additionally, pure LLMs without contextual grounding are highly prone to hallucinations when generating questions.

## Method

The study conducts a comprehensive comparison of two modern approaches:
1. **In-Context Learning (ICL):** Uses the GPT-4 model with few-shot prompts ($k = 3, 5, 7$ examples) to guide question structures without altering model weights.
2. **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG):** Uses the BART-large model combined with a FAISS retrieval module to search for the most relevant textbook resources from the NCERT database, which are then appended to the prompt context.
3. **Hybrid Model (Main proposed method):** Combines the strengths of both. First, the system retrieves the top $k=5$ relevant textbook segments using FAISS to enrich the input context (like RAG), then passes this context along with few-shot prompts ($k=5$ examples) to GPT-4 (like ICL) to generate the final questions.

The researchers compared these models with baselines fine-tuned directly on the target dataset, including T5-large and BART-large.

## Dataset

Uses the **EduProbe** dataset containing **3,502 question-answer pairs** compiled from National Council of Educational Research and Training (NCERT) textbooks for grades 6 to 12. Subject distribution includes:
- History: 858 pairs.
- Geography: 861 pairs.
- Economics: 802 pairs.
- Environmental Studies: 606 pairs.
- Science: 375 pairs.

## Evaluation

The study combines two evaluation methods:
1. **Automated Evaluation:** Measures linguistic quality using BLEU-4, ROUGE-L, METEOR, ChRF, and BERTScore (using the SummEval package).
2. **Human Evaluation:** A panel of 3 high school teachers and 2 students cross-evaluated 1,400 questions generated by 7 different configurations. Questions were rated on a 5-point Likert scale across 5 pedagogical criteria: Grammaticality, Appropriateness (semantic suitability independent of context), Relevance (contextual alignment), Complexity (cognitive depth), and Answerability (ability to answer using the provided text). Rater agreement was validated using Fleiss's Kappa.

## Results

1. **Automated Evaluation:** The ICL method with 7-shot prompts achieved the highest automated metrics (ROUGE-L: 55.95, METEOR: 34.62, BERTScore: 75.92). ICL with 5-shot prompts achieved the highest BLEU-4 (22.87). Pure RAG had relatively lower automated scores because retrieving external text segments sometimes introduced irrelevant content. However, both RAG and the Hybrid Model significantly outperformed the fine-tuned baselines (T5, BART).
2. **Human Evaluation:** The **Hybrid Model** achieved the highest scores and completely dominated on almost all critical criteria: Grammaticality (Gramm = 4.84), Appropriateness (Appr = 4.74), Relevance (Rel = 4.25), and Complexity (Complexity = 4.02). The ICL 7-shot model led in answerability (Answ = 3.31 vs. 3.20 for the Hybrid Model). Fleiss's Kappa ranged from 0.45 to 0.51, representing moderate agreement among teachers and students.

## Limitations

- **Noise from Retrieval:** RAG and the Hybrid Model are prone to performance drops if the FAISS module retrieves off-topic or duplicate materials, reducing question Answerability.
- **Limited Scope of Context:** The experiments were conducted only on NCERT school textbooks with relatively simple structures, and have not yet been evaluated on complex university-level curricula.
- **Lack of Direct Review:** The system does not integrate a direct review or edit interface for teachers before publishing questions to an LMS.

## Relevance to our topic

- Confirms that a **Hybrid model (RAG combined with Few-shot prompting)** is the optimal solution for an LMS generating quizzes based on lecture slides/materials, producing questions that are both context-rich and cognitively complex.
- Provides a standard pedagogical evaluation framework (5-criteria rubric: grammaticality, appropriateness, relevance, complexity, answerability) to apply to the empirical evaluation phase of our research.

## Possible improvement

- **Advanced RAG Upgrades:** Instead of basic FAISS on raw text, apply slide-structured chunking and integrate Re-ranking to increase the accuracy of retrieved information and reduce noise.
- **CLO Alignment:** Adjust the few-shot examples in the Hybrid Model's prompt to directly reflect the cognitive levels required by the selected Course Learning Outcomes (CLOs).
